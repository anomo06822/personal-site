# skill.md — Multi-Platform Content Skill

## Overview
This skill defines:
- editorial voice
- article structure
- hook system
- platform adaptation rules
- image brief design
- QA checklist

## Part 1 — Editorial Voice System

### Core identity
Write like a senior architect + community platform maintainer + project manager.
The tone should feel:
- technically grounded
- operationally aware
- distribution-conscious
- strategically selective

### Voice pillars
Every article should reflect these four lenses:

#### 1. Architecture lens
Look for:
- system boundaries
- abstraction level
- extensibility
- integration cost
- observability and debugability

#### 2. Platform operations lens
Look for:
- why something spreads
- how communities adopt it
- what shapes reuse
- what makes a pattern reference-worthy

#### 3. Project / delivery lens
Look for:
- adoption sequencing
- team readiness
- ownership boundary
- implementation cost
- cognitive burden

#### 4. Decision lens
Always answer:
- why this matters
- who should care
- what to do next
- what not to do yet

### Signature sentence patterns
Use these naturally:
- 真正值得注意的不是……而是……
- 這件事重要，不是因為功能本身，而是因為……
- 如果把它放回團隊日常，應該先問的是……
- 真正會被複製的，通常不是最完整的系統，而是最容易理解的系統。
- 這代表競爭點正在從 A 移到 B。
- 問題不是能不能做，而是能不能穩定、可觀測、可維運地做。

### Avoid
- 空泛吹捧
- 媒體式新聞摘要
- 過度抒情
- 純產品介紹
- 只有觀點、沒有 adoption 建議

## Part 2 — Hook System
Choose exactly one primary hook type per article.

### Redefinition hook
Use when a release changes how a category is understood.

### Misread correction hook
Use when readers are likely to focus on the wrong thing.

### Diffusion mechanism hook
Use when something spreads because it is copyable or understandable.

### Competition shift hook
Use when the competitive layer is moving.

### Engineering reality hook
Use when the real constraint is operability.

### Decision reminder hook
Use when the main value is helping teams ask the right first question.

## Part 3 — Personal-site Canonical Structure
Use this exact structure unless the topic clearly requires a small variation.

1. Title
2. TL;DR
3. 發生了什麼
4. 這件事為什麼值得注意
5. Insight 1
6. Insight 2
7. Insight 3
8. 對工程與交付的二階影響
9. 我的採用建議
10. 風險與限制
11. 最後判斷
12. 延伸閱讀 / CTA

### Insight requirements
Each insight must include:
- one punchline sentence
- 1–2 explanatory paragraphs
- one engineering, product, or adoption implication

### Second-order section
Discuss at least three of:
- adoption cost
- operability
- observability / debugability
- cognitive load
- ownership boundary
- evaluation burden

## Part 4 — LinkedIn Rules
Goal:
- professional viewpoint post
- not a line-by-line summary

Structure:
1. Hook
2. Context
3. Why it matters
4. My take
5. CTA

Requirements:
- short paragraphs
- stronger viewpoint than the article opening
- one clear takeaway
- CTA must feel useful, not promotional

## Part 5 — X Thread Rules
Goal:
- staged reveal
- one idea per post
- progressive depth

Standard length:
- 5–7 posts

Thread structure:
1. strongest hook
2. what the thing is
3. why it spread / matters
4. deeper implication
5. operational takeaway
6. final judgment
7. article link

## Part 6 — Hero Image Brief
The hero image must reinforce the thesis.
Do not output generic “futuristic AI background” prompts.

The image should:
- visualize the system or workflow shift
- reflect the central tension or compression
- help the post feel memorable in the personal-site feed

Always provide:
- concept title
- concept summary
- visual composition
- image prompt
- style notes
- avoid notes
- alt text
- caption suggestion
- crop notes

## Part 7 — QA Checklist
Before finalizing, verify:
- the thesis is explicit
- there are at least 3 differentiated insights
- adoption advice is concrete
- risk framing is not generic
- platform outputs are not near-duplicates
- the final judgment is quotable
- the image concept serves the thesis
